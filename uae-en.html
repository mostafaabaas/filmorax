Guide

Entering the UAE market with video

A brand entering the UAE market with a commercial needs three things before any filming: a film that speaks the language of the market (Arabic in a Gulf dialect understood in Dubai and Abu Dhabi, with an English version where needed), filming in locations that genuinely resemble life for the UAE audience, and written usage rights covering the UAE and the Gulf before filming. A film that looks imported from another market reads as a foreign ad; a film that looks like it comes from inside the market reads as a recommendation.

Why is the UAE a different market?

The UAE is two markets in one: Dubai, a city of trade, tourism and residence, and Abu Dhabi, a city of institutions and families — and the audience in both is a mix of Emiratis, Arab residents and expatriates who live in English. And the production-quality bar there is high: the audience watches world-class commercials every day, so a weak film is not only compared with your local competitor, but with everything the audience watches. Competition in e-commerce, real estate, fintech apps and retail is strong, and a good film there is an entry investment, not a production cost.

Dialect and language: who speaks in the film?

Gulf Arabic is the language of recommendation in the UAE: viewers trust a creator speaking their dialect faster. And the English version is not a literal translation; it is a second version with its own treatment, reaching the resident audience. The decision is built on the placement: social platforms in Arabic first, websites and public places in both versions, with written subtitles where needed. And creator selection happens by category and dialect before follower count — because one interested follower in Dubai is worth more than a thousand general followers.

Locations and context: filming in the UAE or from Cairo?

Remote production runs in four steps:

  • The brief is written online: one message, approved product facts, and a never-say list
  • Filming happens in Egypt or in the UAE, with creators who live in the market itself
  • Review, editing and platform versions run in Cairo with one team
  • The delivery list is written before filming: durations, versions and languages are an agreement, not an assumption

And when the film needs real UAE locations — a mall, a beach, or a residential neighbourhood in Dubai or Abu Dhabi — we film them there; the product is shipped to us, and filming happens with a crew that knows the market. The decision is taken at the brief stage, not on the shoot day.

Usage rights: the film is one thing, the faces in it are another

Paying for production is not owning every use of the film forever. Talent image and voice rights are licensed separately from the film itself: the media (TV, digital/social, out-of-home, in-store, cinema), the territory (the UAE and the Gulf), the duration, and category exclusivity. Music and stock carry their own licence terms. Usage is written before casting — because renegotiating after a successful campaign costs far more.

Measurement: how do you know the entry worked?

Measurement is built before publishing, not after it: trackable links and codes per creator and per city; and indicators agreed in advance: view completion rate, product-page visits from campaign links, orders attributed by codes and UTM, and the volume of messages and enquiries from the new market. And Dubai and Abu Dhabi are measured separately — because what works in one city may not work in the other.

FAQ

Before you start

The questions brands ask most before entering the UAE market with video — with direct answers.

Does filming have to happen in the UAE itself?

Not necessarily. Production is planned in Cairo and filmed in Egypt or in the UAE, depending on what the film needs: you ship the product to us, and filming happens with creators who live in the market itself. And when the film needs real UAE locations, we film them in Dubai or Abu Dhabi — the decision is taken at the brief stage, not on the shoot day.

Is the film in Arabic or in English?

In both: Gulf Arabic understood in Dubai and Abu Dhabi is the language of recommendation, and the English version reaches the resident audience that lives in English. The decision is built on the placement: social platforms in Arabic first, websites and public places in both versions, with written subtitles where needed.

Can the video run as a paid ad?

Yes — on two conditions: written usage rights agreed before filming that cover advertising use, duration and territory (the UAE and the Gulf), and whitelisting permission from the creator's own account when the video is published from their account. Without those two conditions the video stays organic content only.
