Guide

From idea to screen in six stages

A good Egyptian commercial does not start with the camera; it starts with a page. A written brief with one objective and one message, then a treatment and script the client approves, then casting with real faces from the market, then a disciplined shoot day, then editing that builds the film, then organised delivery in every format. Every stage closes before the next one opens — because a postponed decision costs more at the next stage.

The brief: where does the commercial start?

A shoot day is priced, cast and scheduled from the brief. A good brief carries one objective and one message the crew can test every scene against, final placements decided first — because they set the formats and durations — a written deliverables list: durations, versions and languages, not assumptions, and a declared approval chain: who signs off treatment, casting and final cut. And every missing answer in the brief becomes an expensive decision taken on the shoot day itself.

Idea and script: the treatment before the camera

The objective becomes a production document: a treatment explaining the idea, the scenes and the rhythm, then a script the client approves first. And the language is decided here, not in editing: Egyptian Arabic is the language of recommendation for an Egyptian audience — viewers trust a creator speaking their dialect faster — and the Modern Standard Arabic or English version is a second version with its own treatment where the placement needs it. And the story is built on the life of the Egyptian audience itself: situations they know, places they live in, words they hear every day — not scenes imported from another market.

Casting: real faces from the market

An Egyptian commercial is believed through its faces. Casting picks real creators and actors who live in the Egyptian market: selection by category, role and dialect before follower counts — because one interested follower in Cairo is worth more than a thousand general followers. And talent image and voice rights are licensed separately from the film itself: the media (TV, digital/social, out-of-home, in-store, cinema), the territory, the duration, and category exclusivity — and usage is written before casting, because renegotiating after a successful campaign costs far more.

The shoot day: a temporary factory in Cairo

A full shoot day is a temporary factory running 12 hours: before the first shot there is setup, lighting and rehearsal, the schedule is ordered by location and daylight, not script order, and the full crew — direction, camera, lighting, sound, art and cast — works under a call sheet that is the law of the day, where overtime is a decision, not a drift. And locations are chosen to resemble the life of the Egyptian audience: a residential neighbourhood, a shop, or a street the viewer knows — in Cairo and Giza, or at the brand's own location. And after the last shot there is no film — only raw material backed up twice before anyone leaves the location, and the film is built from it in post.

Editing and post: the film is built after filming

The film itself is built in post: editing that orders the scenes on rhythm, grading that unifies the image, sound and sound design and licensed music, graphics and written subtitles where needed. And review rounds run with the client on declared rounds: a first version, then written notes, then a final version — no open-ended reviews without an end. And the short social-platform versions are built from the same master, not filmed again: the same film, at each platform's rhythm and size.

Delivery: a version for every screen

Delivery is organised and written before filming: a master for TV, short versions for social platforms, horizontal and vertical versions by placement, with written subtitles where needed and the licence files. And measurement is built before publishing, not after it: trackable links and codes, and indicators agreed in advance — view completion rate, product-page visits from campaign links, orders attributed by codes, and the volume of messages and enquiries. And TV and social are measured separately — because what works on one screen may not work on the other.

FAQ

Before you start

The questions Egyptian brands ask most before producing a commercial — with direct answers.

Does filming have to happen in Cairo?

Not necessarily. Production is planned in Cairo and filmed in Cairo and Giza or at the brand's own location, depending on what the film needs: locations are chosen to genuinely resemble the life of the Egyptian audience, and the decision is taken at the brief stage, not on the shoot day.

Is the film in Egyptian Arabic or in Modern Standard Arabic?

Egyptian Arabic first: Egyptian Arabic is the language of recommendation for an Egyptian audience — viewers trust a creator speaking their dialect faster. The Modern Standard Arabic or English version is a second version with its own treatment where the placement needs it, with written subtitles where needed.

What delivery formats do we receive?

An organised delivery list written before filming: a master for TV, short versions for social platforms, horizontal and vertical versions by placement, with written subtitles where needed and the licence files. The delivery list is an agreement before filming, not an assumption.
